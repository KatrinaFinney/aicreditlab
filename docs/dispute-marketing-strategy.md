# AI CreditLab: DIY dispute strategy positioning
Date: October 14, 2026

## Position
AI CreditLab helps people doing their own credit repair take the guesswork out of preparing disputes about personal credit report mistakes. Build a starting plan, prepare a fact-based letter, and track the next move. Users review and send; Plus adds AI-assisted wording.

Category descriptor: DIY credit repair and dispute preparation tools.
Campaign line: Credit disputes. Less guesswork. More game plan.
Supporting line: Your report. Your facts. Your next move.

## Light competitive research
- Dovly markets automated electronic TransUnion disputes alongside credit building and protection: https://www.dovly.com/repair-credit/
- Credit Versio already markets DIY letters and dispute strategy, including report imports and results tracking: https://www.creditversio.com/ and https://www.creditversio.com/how-it-works
- Dispute Beast also markets explanations and tailored letters: https://disputebeast.com/vs/credit-versio

Implication: AI letters and dispute strategy are already established features. This is a proposed positioning opportunity, not a claim of an uncontested market or proven superiority. Win attention through transparent preparation, understandable steps, and customer control. Validate the angle with real user behavior.

## Blue ocean choices
Eliminate: deletion guarantees, score promises, shame, aggressive dispute-everything messaging, fabricated testimonials.
Reduce: broad all-in-one credit claims, jargon, AI hype, emphasis on letter volume.
Raise: specificity, plain-language explanations, visible product samples, honest feature limits, user control.
Create: a recognizable “next move” editorial framework: issue → facts → requested correction → review/send → response.

Today's product supports starting plans, personal templates, AI drafts on Plus, progress tracking and manual dispute statuses. It does not automatically inspect reports, mail letters, determine legal eligibility, monitor bureau responses, or intelligently sequence disputes. The three-question plan is a starting plan, not a comprehensive personalized dispute analysis.

## Audience and value
Primary: adults who see something questionable on their personal credit report and feel overwhelmed about what to say or do next.
Trigger: wrong balance, duplicate entry, unfamiliar account, or another specific suspected mistake.
Job: turn uncertainty into a clear, reviewable next action.
Emotional promise: clarity and agency without judgment.
Paid value: convenience of AI-assisted drafting and additional organization tools; not access to the right to dispute.

## Copy system
Hero: Credit disputes. Less guesswork. More game plan.
Body: Take the guesswork out of disputing credit report mistakes. Build a starting plan, put your facts into a clear letter, and keep track of your next move. You review and send. Plus adds AI-assisted drafting.
Primary CTA: Build my free plan.
Secondary CTA: Preview a dispute letter.
Workflow: Get your bearings → Put your facts to work → Review. Send. Stay organized.
Trust line: AI assists. You call the shots.
Closing: Your report. Your facts. Your next move.

Use short sentences, direct verbs and concrete situations. Let the rhythm feel contemporary without slang that trivializes financial stress. Keep “credit repair” in discovery copy; make disputes the main story. Keep business credit as a secondary, separately described use case because letters are personal-only.

## Trust in design
Keep the established teal accents, professional typography and readable dark palette.
Show a labeled fictional example of the workflow near the hero, not a simulated score gain.
Place the free allowance and no-card language beside the CTA.
Show template vs AI distinctions directly in pricing.
Keep privacy, help, self-send responsibilities, and realistic outcome language easy to find.
Use genuine testimonials only with consent and context. Avoid unsupported security badges.

## Content and acquisition
Repeat three content pillars:
1. Spot the issue: “Wrong balance? Start with the payment records.”
2. Make the case: fictional before/after examples of vague versus specific explanations, without inventing facts.
3. Own the next move: reviewing drafts, keeping copies, and organizing responses.

Starter hooks:
- “Your dispute starts before the letter.”
- “Know what needs correcting. Then put it into words.”
- “A wrong balance needs a clear explanation.”
- “AI can help with the wording. The facts come from you.”
- “The blank page is optional.”

Start with short TikTok/Reels screen demos, companion text posts, and search pages answering specific error questions. Each piece points to the existing letter preview or free plan. Show preparation, not promised deletion results.

## Four-week validation
Week 1: release the homepage message; record a baseline for hero CTA clicks, preview visits and completed plans.
Week 2: publish three issue-led demos (balance, duplicate, unfamiliar account).
Week 3: publish a fictional letter walkthrough and explain exactly what Plus adds.
Week 4: compare qualified sign-ups and first completed letter downloads; interview five users about what they believed the tool would do.

Primary measure: new users who prepare their first reviewed letter/download.
Supporting measures: preview-to-plan clicks, plan completion, sign-up completion, first download, paid upgrade when checkout is available.
Track aggregate events without names, report facts, balances, or letter contents. Existing funnel analytics can provide part of the baseline; verify event coverage before claiming full attribution.
Judge trust through comprehension: can users explain who sends letters, what AI does, and what outcomes are not promised?

## Product roadmap to make the position stronger
Future work, not current marketing claims:
- structured error intake and an evidence checklist;
- editable dispute action plans linked to each issue;
- response-aware follow-up guidance;
- reminders and a clearer record of next actions.
Do not advertise these as shipped.

## Accuracy guardrails
People can dispute errors directly for free. Explain the paid tool's convenience.
Focus on information believed inaccurate or incomplete. Accurate negative information generally cannot be removed through a dispute.
CFPB explains including the error, why it is wrong, and supporting documents:
https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-dispute-an-error-on-my-credit-report-en-314/
https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/is-it-possible-to-remove-accurate-negative-information-from-my-credit-report-en-1249/
Free: 30 personal templates, three template downloads per UTC calendar month, one saved plan, tracking.
Plus: $9.99/month, multiple plans, unlimited personal template downloads, five AI-assisted personal drafts per UTC calendar month.
Keep the existing checkout availability gate. Do not imply paid AI is included free.
